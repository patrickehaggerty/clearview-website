Many small business owners assume hiring a payroll company means someone else calculates employee paychecks.

While payroll processing is an important part of the service, full-service payroll and HR support can provide significantly more value.

For businesses without a dedicated human resources department, outsourcing these responsibilities can simplify operations and allow employees to focus on their primary jobs.

## What Does Full-Service Payroll Include?

Services vary by provider, but a comprehensive payroll solution may include several important functions.

### 1. Payroll Processing

Payroll processing involves calculating employee earnings, deductions, and net pay.

This may include:

·       Regular wages and salaries

·       Overtime payments

·       Bonuses and commissions

·       Employee deductions

·       Paid time off

·       Direct deposits

Having an established payroll process helps businesses maintain consistent pay schedules.

### 2. Payroll Tax Administration

Employers are responsible for various federal and state payroll tax obligations.

Payroll providers may assist with calculating, depositing, and filing:

·       Federal income tax withholding

·       Social Security and Medicare taxes

·       Federal unemployment taxes

·       State income tax withholding

·       State unemployment insurance contributions

It's important to confirm exactly which tax services are included in your agreement. Employers generally retain responsibility for ensuring their payroll tax obligations are satisfied, even when using a third-party provider.

### 3. Employee Onboarding

Hiring an employee involves more than adding someone to payroll.

An organized onboarding process may include collecting employee information, tax forms, direct deposit instructions, and employment-related documentation.

Online onboarding tools can help employers streamline these processes and reduce paperwork.

### 4. Time and Attendance Management

Timekeeping is particularly important for businesses with hourly employees.

Integrated time and attendance systems can help track hours worked, support overtime calculations, and reduce duplicate data entry.

Accurate timekeeping also provides important records for payroll and wage-and-hour compliance.

### 5. Benefits and Employee Deductions

Employers may need to coordinate payroll deductions for health insurance, retirement plans, and other employee benefits.

When payroll and benefits information is properly coordinated, businesses can reduce administrative work and improve record accuracy.

### 6. HR Support

Payroll and HR responsibilities frequently overlap.

Depending on the provider and service agreement, HR support may include employee onboarding assistance, access to HR resources, payroll-related documentation, and help coordinating routine administrative requirements.

Employers should confirm whether specialized employment law guidance is included or requires assistance from an attorney or other qualified professional.

## Why Combining Payroll and HR Services Makes Sense

When payroll and HR activities are handled through disconnected systems, businesses can experience duplicate work, inconsistent records, and communication problems.

A coordinated approach helps reduce these challenges.

For example, when an employee changes their pay rate, updates their benefits, or leaves the company, that information needs to flow accurately into payroll.
