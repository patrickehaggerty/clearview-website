## Why Is Restaurant Accounting Different From Other Businesses?

Restaurants face accounting challenges that many other industries don't experience.

Daily sales may include cash, credit cards, online orders, third-party delivery platforms, gift cards, and tips. Each payment method must be properly accounted for to maintain accurate financial records.

Restaurants also have fluctuating food costs, labor expenses, and inventory needs that can significantly affect profitability.

Without accurate bookkeeping, owners may not realize how quickly small discrepancies and increasing expenses are affecting their bottom line.

A restaurant-focused accounting provider can help organize these transactions and provide financial reporting that makes it easier to understand business performance.

## 1. Restaurant Payroll and Tip Reporting

Payroll is one of the most complicated administrative responsibilities for restaurant owners.

Restaurants frequently employ a combination of hourly employees, tipped employees, salaried managers, part-time workers, and employees working multiple positions at different pay rates.

Restaurant payroll may involve:

·       Tracking regular hours and overtime

·       Processing credit card and cash tips

·       Managing tip pooling and tip sharing arrangements

·       Calculating payroll taxes

·       Managing multiple pay rates

·       Processing employee bonuses

·       Handling employee onboarding and terminations

·       Preparing year-end W-2 forms

Tip reporting and payroll compliance are especially important in the restaurant industry.

Federal and state wage requirements, tip credits, overtime rules, and recordkeeping requirements can create additional challenges for restaurant owners.

Properly configured payroll systems can help restaurants consistently record employee compensation and support compliance with applicable requirements.

## 2. Daily Sales Reconciliation and POS Integration

Restaurants process numerous transactions every day, often through multiple payment methods and sales channels.

Point-of-sale systems such as Toast, Square, and other restaurant platforms provide valuable sales information, but those reports still need to be accurately reflected in the restaurant's accounting records.

Restaurant bookkeeping should account for:

·       Cash sales

·       Credit and debit card transactions

·       Sales taxes collected

·       Employee tips

·       Gift card sales and redemptions

·       Refunds and discounts

·       Delivery platform transactions

·       Merchant processing fees

One common bookkeeping mistake is recording bank deposits as total restaurant sales.

Because deposits may be reduced by credit card processing fees, tips, or third-party delivery charges, the amount deposited into the bank may not equal the restaurant's gross revenue.

Proper reconciliation helps ensure that revenue, expenses, tips, taxes, and deposits are recorded appropriately.

### Why Does This Matter?

Without accurate reconciliation, restaurant owners may overstate or understate revenue, misclassify expenses, or have difficulty identifying missing deposits.

A consistent daily or periodic sales reconciliation process helps produce more reliable financial information.

## 4. Understanding Food Costs and Labor Costs

Two of the most important expenses for restaurant profitability are food costs and labor costs.

Even when a restaurant generates strong sales, rising costs can quickly reduce profits.

### Food Cost Percentage

Food cost percentage is commonly calculated as:

Food Cost Percentage = Cost of Food Sold ÷ Food Sales × 100

For example, if a restaurant generates $100,000 in food sales and incurs $30,000 in food costs associated with those sales, its food cost percentage is 30%.

Tracking this percentage over time can help owners identify potential issues with purchasing, menu pricing, waste, portion control, or inventory management.

### Labor Cost Percentage

Labor cost percentage is commonly calculated as:

Labor Cost Percentage = Total Labor Costs ÷ Total Sales × 100

Total labor costs should be defined consistently and may include wages, employer payroll taxes, benefits, and other employee-related expenses.

For example, if total sales are $100,000 and labor costs are $32,000, the labor cost percentage is 32%.

Both metrics should be evaluated based on the restaurant's concept, operating model, historical performance, and financial goals.

### Why Financial Reporting Matters

Restaurant owners should not have to wait until tax season to find out whether their business is profitable.

Consistent financial reporting can help owners evaluate:

·       Food and beverage costs

·       Labor expenses

·       Prime cost (cost of goods sold plus labor costs)

·       Gross profit

·       Operating expenses

·       Net income

·       Cash flow

·       Performance by location or department

These reports can provide the information needed to make better staffing, purchasing, and pricing decisions.

## 6. Should Restaurants Outsource Their Bookkeeping and Payroll?

Many restaurant owners initially handle bookkeeping and payroll themselves or assign those responsibilities to a general manager or office employee.

However, as the business grows, these tasks can take valuable time away from operations.

Outsourcing may be beneficial when:

·       The owner spends too much time managing financial records.

·       Payroll and tip reporting are becoming complicated.

·       Bank accounts and POS reports aren't reconciled consistently.

·       The restaurant needs better monthly financial statements.

·       Sales tax preparation is consuming valuable administrative time.

·       The business is expanding or opening additional locations.

Outsourced accounting can provide financial oversight without requiring the restaurant to hire an entire internal accounting department.
