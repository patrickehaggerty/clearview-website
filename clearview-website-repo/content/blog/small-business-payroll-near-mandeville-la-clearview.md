# What Small Business Payroll Actually Includes

Payroll is more than cutting checks. A complete [payroll process](clearviewas.com/payroll/) includes:

•    Calculating gross pay for salaried, hourly, overtime, commission, and bonus earnings

•    Withholding taxes for federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and Louisiana income tax

•    Processing deductions for health insurance, retirement plans, garnishments, and other benefits

•    Paying employees by direct deposit or check

•    Depositing payroll taxes with the IRS and Louisiana Department of Revenue on the correct schedule

•    Filing returns, including quarterly Form 941, Louisiana withholding returns, and quarterly unemployment wage reports to the Louisiana Workforce Commission

•    Year-end reporting, including W-2s, W-3s, 1099s, and annual reconciliations

•    Recording payroll in your accounting system so your financial statements reflect true labor costs

Miss any one of those and the problem tends to show up months later as a penalty notice.

# Louisiana Payroll Rules Every Northshore Employer Should Know

## Flat State Income Tax

Since January 1, 2025, Louisiana has used a flat 3% individual income tax rate instead of graduated brackets. Employees should complete a current Louisiana Form L-4 so withholding is accurate.

## Unemployment Insurance

Louisiana employers pay state unemployment insurance (SUI) on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages in 2026. Your rate is assigned by the Louisiana Workforce Commission and changes annually, so your payroll system needs to be updated when your new rate notice arrives.

## No State Minimum Wage

Louisiana hasn't adopted its own minimum wage, so the federal minimum of $7.25 per hour applies. Federal overtime rules under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) still apply as well, including time-and-a-half for non-exempt employees who work more than 40 hours in a week.

## New Hire Reporting and Final Pay

Every new hire or rehire must be reported to the Louisiana Directory of New Hires within 20 days. When an employee leaves, Louisiana requires final wages to be paid by the next regular payday or within 15 days, whichever comes first.

# The Most Common Small Business Payroll Mistakes

When Northshore businesses come to us for payroll help, these are the issues we see most often.

## Misclassifying Workers

Calling someone an independent contractor when they function like an employee is one of the most expensive mistakes a small business can make. The IRS looks at behavioral control, financial control, and the relationship between the parties. If you set their schedule, provide their tools, and they work only for you, they're likely an employee.

## Missing Tax Deposit Deadlines

Payroll tax deposits run on their own schedule, separate from when you pay employees. Late deposits trigger penalties that add up fast, even when the payroll itself was correct.

## Not Updating Rates and Tables

Tax rates, wage bases, and withholding tables change every year. If your system isn't updated, you could be under-withholding or overpaying all year.

## Payroll That Doesn't Match the Books

Payroll reports say one thing and QuickBooks says another. Employer taxes get lumped into one expense account, benefits deductions don't reconcile to the insurance bill, and liability accounts carry balances nobody can explain. It looks fine until your CPA starts year-end work.

## Handling It All Yourself

Many owners run payroll at 10 p.m. after a full workday. That's when errors happen. It's also time that could go toward growing the business.

# In-House Payroll vs. Outsourced Payroll

Small businesses generally choose among three approaches:

•    Doing it yourself with payroll software. Lowest cost, but you own every setup decision and every notice.

•    Hiring an in-house payroll or office manager. Gives you control, but adds salary and benefits cost, and creates risk when that person is out or leaves.

•    Outsourcing to a payroll firm. A team handles processing, filings, and notices, with a dedicated contact who knows your business.

For most businesses with 5 to 100 employees, outsourcing is the sweet spot. You get professional-level accuracy without adding headcount, and you're not dependent on one person's knowledge.
